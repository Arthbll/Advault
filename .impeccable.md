## Design Context

### Users
Media buyers — performance marketers who manage advertising campaigns across multiple ad networks (ExoClick, TrafficStars, TrafficJunky, PropellerAds, Adsterra). They work at high cadence: checking numbers frequently, making fast decisions, monitoring automation.

**Context of use:** Mostly desktop. Likely evening/night sessions in a dimly lit office or home setup. The dashboard is a "check-in" tool — they glance at what the robot did, act on what needs action, and move on. Speed and clarity of information matter more than exploration.

**Job to be done:** Understand at a glance whether campaigns are healthy, take quick corrective actions, and trust the automation engine to handle the rest.

**Emotional state:** Focused, analytical, sometimes stressed (money at stake). The interface should reduce anxiety, not add to it.

### Brand Personality
**Three words:** Precise. Operator. Confident.

This is a tool for professionals who take their work seriously. Not a toy, not a startup template, not a fintech dashboard clone. It should feel like a piece of equipment — the kind you trust with your money.

**Tone:** Direct, no-nonsense. Information density is a feature, not a problem. The interface respects the user's intelligence.

**Not:** Gamer (no neons), corporate enterprise (no blue-gray sameness), AI-generated SaaS (no gradient text, no purple-to-cyan, no generic card grids).

### Aesthetic Direction
**Theme:** Dark. Always dark. These users are in low-light environments managing campaigns that run 24/7.

**Color philosophy:** 
- Background: `#0d0d10` — near-black with a subtle cool-dark tint
- Surfaces: `#111113`, `#18181b` — lifted slightly from background
- Text: `rgba(255,255,255,0.9)` primary, `rgba(255,255,255,0.5)` secondary, `rgba(255,255,255,0.3)` tertiary
- Accent: White (`#ffffff`) for active states — maximum contrast, clean, no color drama
- Network colors: Each ad network has a distinct, saturated-but-not-neon color (ExoClick amber, TrafficStars violet, TrafficJunky sky, PropellerAds orange, Adsterra cyan)
- Avoid: Pure neon glows, purple-as-a-brand-color, gradients as decoration

**Reference feel:** Somewhere between Linear (speed, precision, keyboard-first) and Bloomberg Terminal (density, trust, operator tool). But warmer than Bloomberg, less playful than Linear.

**Anti-references:** Gamer dashboards with neon borders, generic SaaS with hero gradients, dark mode templates that use purple/cyan as default accent colors.

### Design Principles

1. **Operator-first density** — Show more, not less. Users understand their data. Don't infantilize with oversized cards and huge whitespace.

2. **Trust through precision** — Numbers must be right, and must look right. Tabular numerics, aligned columns, clear labels. Ambiguity = distrust.

3. **Motion serves function** — Animate state changes (pauses, actions, status updates), not decoration. If an animation doesn't communicate something, remove it.

4. **Premium without decoration** — Quality comes from precision of spacing, typography, and color — not from gradients, glows, or patterns. Every element earns its place.

5. **Calm confidence** — The interface should not feel urgent or alarming in its visual design. Even when showing an issue, the presentation is measured. Calm tools make better decisions.
